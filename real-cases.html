DOCUMENTED CASE / 01

Golden State Killer

California, 1970s–1980s

Joseph James DeAngelo was identified in 2018 through investigative genetic genealogy. Prosecutors said he pleaded guilty to 13 murders and 13 kidnappings; he was sentenced in 2020.

Investigation and evidence

A key breakthrough was the use of investigative genetic genealogy alongside traditional investigative work. The case illustrates how older forensic evidence can be revisited with newer methods.

OFFICIAL SOURCE: Contra Costa County District Attorney's Office

DOCUMENTED CASE / 02

The BTK Investigation

Kansas, 1974–2005

The BTK investigation ended with the arrest of Dennis Rader in 2005. Investigators used a combination of renewed leads and digital evidence to identify him.

Investigation and evidence

The case is a reminder that digital traces and investigative follow-up can matter even after many years. Avoid treating a single clue as proof without corroboration.

OFFICIAL SOURCE: Federal Bureau of Investigation

DOCUMENTED CASE / 03

The Unabomber Case

United States, 1978–1996

The FBI-led task force investigated a series of mail and package bombings over nearly two decades. A published manifesto prompted a family member to contact investigators, and writing comparisons helped build the case.

Investigation and evidence

Investigators combined document analysis, family information, forensic work and a search of the suspect's cabin. The FBI records that Theodore Kaczynski was arrested in 1996.

OFFICIAL SOURCE: Federal Bureau of Investigation

DOCUMENTED CASE / 04

Oklahoma City Bombing

Oklahoma City, 1995

The bombing of the Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building killed 168 people. Investigators traced a vehicle identification number from recovered truck parts to a rental facility, helping identify Timothy McVeigh.

Investigation and evidence

The FBI describes how vehicle parts, rental records, witness information and extensive investigative leads contributed to the case. The tragedy caused immense loss of life.

OFFICIAL SOURCE: Federal Bureau of Investigation

DOCUMENTED CASE / 05

Boston Marathon Bombing

Boston, 2013

After the 2013 bombing, investigators released photographs and video of two suspects. The FBI describes a large evidence response involving surveillance footage, public tips and forensic examination.

Investigation and evidence

The FBI reports that investigators processed thousands of pieces of evidence and collected large volumes of digital information. The investigation led to the identification and prosecution of the surviving suspect.

OFFICIAL SOURCE: Federal Bureau of Investigation
